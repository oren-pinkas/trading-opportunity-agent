## Round 1 — Independent research

### Bull (Catalyst-hunter, sonnet)

targeting a USD 7.9B valuation, NYSE ticker JMKE, and a debut targeted for July 30, 2026 — five days
out per the StockTitan reporting (accessed 2026-07-23). This is a textbook IPO-pop setup: a
well-known consumer brand with national retail recognition (unlike a niche industrial or biotech
name), roadshow launched with a defined pricing band, and a hard listing date already circulating in
the press. Franchise/QSR IPOs with strong brand recognition and healthy unit economics narratives
have historically drawn retail demand precisely because the story is easy to understand — "sandwich
chain everyone already eats at goes public" sells itself to generalist investors, which is the kind
of narrative that tends to produce day-one demand imbalances (oversubscription, allocation scarcity)
even though I can't cite a specific first-day return number in advance.

Why this moves the stock: IPO-day price action is driven by underwriter book-building tightness
relative to demand. A named pricing band (21-25) that later prices at or above the top, or upsizes,
is itself a signal of demand strength worth watching for in the next few days. The catalyst here is
discrete and dated (July 30 debut), which is exactly the kind of event-driven, binary-timing setup
that suits a momentum play — get in when the tape confirms direction rather than trying to predict
the pop.

Uncertainty, explicitly: Final IPO price is not yet set (roadshow just launched), so the actual print
price, deal size, and demand could shift the band before pricing. First-day pop/drop is fundamentally
unknowable in advance — this is a bet on the catalyst existing and being tradeable, not on a specific
magnitude.

Proposed action (consistent with lessons 1 & 2): Long JMKE via common stock (no options — too new for
listed/liquid derivatives on debut day). Do NOT schedule an entry at the literal open print. Instead:
(a) first confirm via the price provider that JMKE has a live, confirmed listing timestamp on July 30
before scheduling anything (lesson 2); (b) once confirmed, delay entry 30-60 minutes post-first-print
to trade into a liquid, fillable bar (lesson 1); (c) enter long on that first liquid bar, targeting a
short swing hold (days, not weeks) to capture initial post-IPO momentum, with a stop below the
post-open liquid-window low. Size small given the unknowable pop magnitude and lack of trading
history.

### Bear (Skeptic, sonnet)

The thesis is already priced in. A roadshow isn't a leak — it's a deliberate multi-week marketing
exercise where the company and underwriters walk institutional buyers through the exact numbers a
retail/algo trader would react to. The $21-25 range and $7.9B valuation are the syndicate's
calibrated read of demand after meeting with the accounts that matter. By the time JMKE opens for
retail on July 30, the info edge is gone — any "cheap vs. peers" argument the bull makes was already
tested against real order books during the roadshow.

Valuation risk. $7.9B for a sandwich chain implies a rich multiple versus quick-service comps
(Wingstop, Portillo's, First Watch have all traded at elevated multiples then round-tripped hard once
lockups or growth deceleration hit). Jersey Mike's is a franchise-royalty model — thin owned-store
economics, single-brand, no international diversification, competing in a saturated fast-casual
sandwich category (Subway, Firehouse, Potbelly). Single-concept franchisors carry real re-rating risk
the first time same-store-sales growth disappoints.

IPO-day mechanics work against the bull. Restaurant IPOs (Portillo's, Cava) show a pattern: pop on
day one driven by scarcity/index-inclusion speculation, then fade over weeks as lockup-driven supply
fears build and growth-multiple skeptics get their turn. A single-day long is betting on momentum
that's already anticipated by market makers pricing the open.

Execution/deal risk. Pricing ranges shift late (could reprice above or below $21-25), and roadshows
do get pulled or delayed on soft demand — we have no confirmed final price or firm listing timestamp
yet, only "expected July 30." Per institutional lessons, this opportunity is not tradeable as
specified until: (1) the exchange listing date/time is confirmed and present in the price provider,
and (2) entry is delayed 30-60 min past first print rather than hard-coded to the open.

What the bull likely ignores: brand strength ≠ multiple support; day-one pop is largely mechanical
(scarcity, index funds, underwriter stabilization) not fundamental; and the flip side of "strong
demand" is that if it's genuinely oversubscribed, the easy money already went to roadshow allocants,
not day-one retail buyers.

What would change my mind: a confirmed listing timestamp in the price provider, price near the low
end of $21-25 signaling underwriter caution has already discounted risk, and same-store-sales/unit
economics disclosures in the S-1 that show durable growth beyond the roadshow narrative — plus liquid
post-open price action showing genuine institutional accumulation, not just a mechanical pop.

### Quant (Pragmatist, opus)

Base rates (pattern, not fabricated stats): US large-cap consumer/restaurant IPOs historically show a
positive mean first-day pop from offer price to first print — typically low-to-mid double digits —
but with a median well below the mean and enormous variance: a fat right tail (well-known brands that
double) and a real left tail (broken deals that trade below offer within days). Two decompositions
matter: (1) Offer → first print: where nearly all the historical "pop" lives, captured only by
allocated institutions, not accessible to us. (2) First print → close: roughly a coin flip, slightly
negative-skewed as flippers hit bids — the only leg our execution can touch. Lesson 1 forces entry
into a liquid window 30-60min after first print, structurally excluding leg 1.

EV calculation (entry T+45min, exit same-day close): Generous case: P(up)=0.50 mean +4.0%, P(down)=0.50
mean -4.0%, Gross EV = 0.00%. Honest priors: P(up)=0.45 mean +3.0%, P(down)=0.55 mean -3.5%, Gross EV
= -0.58%. Frictions (entry slippage ~0.35%, exit slippage ~0.25%, fees ~0.05%) ~0.65% round trip. Net
EV = -1.23% (honest case), -0.65% (generous case).

Date-confirmation risk multiplies the above: P(prices on confirmed date, ticker resolvable in
provider) ~0.70, so 30% chance the plan is unfillable — an operational tail loss with no offsetting
upside.

Recommendation: NO-TRADE. Size: 0%. Conditions to revisit: confirmed listing date/time, verified
minute bars present in provider, demonstrated post-first-print directional signal with win rate above
~58% (breakeven given 65bp costs at 4% magnitude).
