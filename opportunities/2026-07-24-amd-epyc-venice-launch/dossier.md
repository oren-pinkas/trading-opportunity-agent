## Scouted 2026-07-24T03:30:53Z

## Researched 2026-07-26T08:15:10Z

No trade. The EPYC Venice / TSMC N2 keynote is a well-telegraphed roadmap event -
Zen 6 on N2 has been on AMD's public roadmap for over a year, and the only sourcing
available was a hobbyist hardware tracker rather than any financial disclosure,
with no design-win data, no shipping timeline, and a typical 6 to 12+ month
announce-to-ship lag. Real price data settled the question: AMD ran plus 7.59 to
7.89 percent on 2026-07-21, the day before the keynote, meaning the market
front-ran the announcement; the stock then round-tripped from a 552.50 close on
07-22 to 520.56 on 07-24, closing on the day's low (minus 4.82 percent
open-to-close), with about 67 percent of the pop already given back and shares
now net 1.94 percent below the pre-runup baseline. That eliminates both legs:
there is no under-reaction left to buy, and no residual hype left to fade - a
short here would be betting on continuation of an exhausted move, not on a
mispricing. Against realized daily vol of about 4.36 to 4.44 percent (about 69 to
70 percent annualized), the long thesis carries an edge to vol ratio of roughly
0.05 to 0.12 sigma, statistically indistinguishable from zero, and a repaired
pre-earnings variant (07-27 entry, 08-03 exit) still only reaches about 0.10
sigma. Compounding this, AMD's Q2 FY26 earnings almost certainly falls inside the
proposed 07-27 to 08-14 hold window - an unhedged plus or minus 8 to 10 percent
binary, entirely unresearched and orthogonal to the Venice story, that is four to
five times larger than the thesis edge itself. Separately, the originally
proposed 2026-08-15 exit is a Saturday and not a valid trading day, a plan defect
that was repairable but is not the reason for the verdict. The verdict rests on
three findings, in order: the edge to vol ratio is effectively zero, the earnings
binary dwarfs the thesis, and the catalyst was demonstrably consumed by the tape
before the hold window opens. Note for the record that this is a judgment about
this specific window and this specific evidence, not a rejection of AMD's
underlying Venice server-share thesis, which remains untested here for lack of
any design-win or shipment data. Full debate: see transcript.md.
