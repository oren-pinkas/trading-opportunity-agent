## Round 1 — Independent Research

### BULL (opening)

BULL — Opening Position: Long YPF ADR

**1. Mechanism: why this moves YPF specifically**

YPF is Argentina's national oil & gas champion — upstream producer plus
refining/downstream. A Brent spike from Mideast escalation hits YPF through three
direct channels, not just narrative:

- **Revenue/margin pass-through on the upstream barrel.** YPF's crude and Vaca
  Muerta shale production is priced off international benchmarks (Brent/WTI-linked
  with local adjustments). A geopolitical supply-shock rally in Brent lifts realized
  prices on every barrel YPF sells, with limited immediate cost offset since lifting
  costs are largely peso-denominated and sticky short-term. That's operating
  leverage working in the bulls' favor.
- **Index-flow amplification.** The Merval's energy-led surge means passive and
  momentum flows are already rotating into the energy complex within the index.
  YPF is the single largest, most liquid energy name in Merval — it captures a
  disproportionate share of that rotation, both from local investors and the ADR
  arb channel in New York.
- **FX/hedge dynamic.** Higher oil revenue = more hard-currency export receipts for
  YPF, a partial natural hedge against peso depreciation risk that has historically
  capped Argentine equity re-ratings.

**2. Evidence in hand**

- Source: RTO "LatAm Pre-Open, Wednesday July 22, 2026" — Merval surge led by
  energy names, Brent jump on Mideast escalation, Argentina outperforming the
  LatAm region.
- Price action: YPF ADR $52.33 (7/22 14:30Z) → $52.9738 (7/23 14:30Z), +1.2% in one
  session — consistent with early momentum building, not yet a blow-off top.

**3. Proposed trade**

- **Direction:** Long.
- **Instrument:** YPF ADR (NYSE-listed).
- **Entry:** Now, near $52.97–$53.50 on continuation confirmation.
- **Exit/impact window:** Target into 2026-07-31 — an 8-day momentum window; take
  profits into strength if Brent momentum stalls or de-escalation headlines hit;
  trailing stop rather than a hard target.
- **Why now:** The move is one day old. Merval energy leadership plus a genuine
  external commodity catalyst is the highest-quality setup.

**4. Confidence: Moderate (6/10)**

What would raise conviction: (a) an actual Brent price print/level, (b) confirmation
the Mideast escalation is intensifying rather than fading, (c) YPF-specific volume
data confirming this isn't just index-composition drift.

### BEAR (opening)

BEAR — Round 1 Opening: Fade or Skip YPF Long

**1. Already priced in, stale by the time we can act.**
YPF went $52.33 → $52.97 (+1.2%) over 24 hours — well within normal daily noise for
YPF, which regularly produces 2-3% single-day swings on nothing at all. By the time
this dossier was scouted (7/23, 9:13am) the print already happened. There is no edge
in chasing a move that's already in the tape.

**2. What reverses it.**
- **Oil mean-reversion:** Geopolitical spikes in Brent are notoriously fast to fade
  absent an actual physical supply disruption. Headline-driven escalation premium
  typically unwinds within days to weeks once the market confirms no barrels are
  actually off the market.
- **Argentina-specific overhangs:** Burford Capital / Petersen litigation (the
  ~USD 16B expropriation case, 2nd Circuit appeal and turnover-of-shares ruling
  still working through US courts). FX/capital-controls risk under Milei's
  program. State-ownership overhang (YPF is majority state-owned, subject to
  policy risk on export taxes/price controls on domestic fuel).
- **De-escalation risk:** Any diplomatic step-down could snap Brent back down in
  hours.

**3. What the bull is ignoring.**
The bull thesis conflates "Merval outperformed LatAm" with "YPF has a durable,
tradeable edge." A 1.2%-over-24h move gives essentially no risk/reward asymmetry.

**4. Proposed action: SKIP.**
Confidence: high (75%) that this specific setup, at this specific price, is not an
actionable long.

## Round 2 — Rebuttal

### BULL (rebuttal)

**1. Concessions:** 0.16x six-day sigma is genuinely weak confirmation; I was too
quick to call a 1.2% print a "catalyst" on its own. Most oil-geopolitical spikes
without physical supply disruption do fade in 1-2 weeks.

**2. Push back:** The base-rate model ignores two YPF-specific transmission
channels. First, YPF's Vaca Muerta export ramp means realized price pass-through is
structurally larger than YPF's historical 5-year Brent beta (measured before the
export buildout). Second, Merval energy-sector rotation flows tend to lag the spot
oil move by 2-4 trading days — the 1.2% move could be the first tick of
flow-driven follow-through, not the whole story already priced. On litigation/FX:
real risks, but orthogonal to the trade thesis — Burford/Petersen is a
multi-year, low-probability-per-week tail event with no specific catalyst date in
window; FX/capital-controls risk has been a known, roughly-priced overhang for
months. Arguments for smaller size, not zero size.

**3. What changes my mind:** (a) Brent gives back more than half the spike within
2 trading days; (b) YPF ADR closes below $51.80; (c) any Petersen ruling or
capital-controls headline before 07-31 — exit immediately.

**4. Revised stance:** Confidence down to 4/10. Small starter position only, tight
stop at $51.80, no add unless Merval energy-sector volume confirms rotation within
2 sessions.

## Round 3 — Convergence (Synthesis)

### HYPOTHESIS

**Statement:** The +1.23% one-day move in YPF ($52.33 → $52.9738) on a Brent
geopolitical-premium spike does not constitute a tradeable signal within the
6-trading-day window to 2026-07-31. The move is 0.16x six-day sigma —
indistinguishable from noise — and therefore fails as confirmation of either the
oil-pass-through mechanism or the Merval energy-rotation flow mechanism the bull
proposes. Both proposed mechanisms are directionally plausible but not yet
observable: pass-through is a level effect already reflected in price, and
rotation-lag is an untested assertion. Against that unconfirmed edge sits an
asymmetric, gap-shaped left tail specific to YPF (Petersen/Burford ~USD 16B
judgment on 2nd Circuit appeal, FX/capital-controls policy risk, export-tax/
state-ownership risk) that a trailing stop structurally cannot control. Quant's EV
of -0.36% gross / -0.51% net after 15bps round-trip is the binding number.

**Direction:** no-trade
**Confidence:** 79

no-trade by genuinely independent routes — structural/idiosyncratic tail
